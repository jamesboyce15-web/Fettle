# Fettle evidence review: health, fire and money

Checked on 12 November 2026. Covers the 23 website guides, the plan and digital book in the app, and the printed workbook.

**The rule:** every piece of advice must trace to an official source or a recognised charity. Where it didn't, it was corrected, re-sourced, softened or removed.

**What this is and isn't.** This is a line-by-line check of the content against published guidance, done by an AI assistant. It is not a sign-off by a registered clinician, fire officer or welfare rights adviser. The last section lists what a professional should still look at.

## Corrections that mattered most

1. **Hypothermia.** We said "call 999 if they're seriously unwell, or 111 for advice". The NHS says hypothermia is an emergency: call 999 or go to A&E. Fixed everywhere, and added the NHS "don'ts" (no hot bath, no hot water bottle, don't rub limbs).
2. **Heat exhaustion.** We said "get medical help" if someone isn't better in 30 minutes. The NHS says call 999. Fixed in the guide (the workbook already said 999).
3. **Babies and hats.** The digital book said to keep a baby "in layers, with a hat". The Lullaby Trust says babies don't need hats indoors and heads should be uncovered for sleep. Fixed in the app, guides and workbook.
4. **Spare medicines.** We told people to "get a week's spare medicine" from the GP or pharmacist. No official source says this, and GPs don't normally issue it. Be Ready's advice is to order repeat prescriptions in good time and keep a written list of medicines and doses. Changed everywhere.
5. **Awaab's Law and temporary accommodation.** We said the time limits "don't cover temporary or supported accommodation". The government's tenant guidance says it depends on whether you have a tenancy or a licence. Telling people they had no rights was wrong. Fixed.
6. **Carbon monoxide "can kill in minutes".** No official source uses that. The gas networks say it "can kill quickly without warning". Changed.
7. **Water notices.** Our descriptions of "do not drink" and "do not use" notices had no national source. Rewritten to what can be supported, and to follow the notice.

## What a professional should still look at

**A pharmacist or GP**
- The homemade rehydration recipe. It is quoted exactly from the Chief Medical Officers' script, but it has more salt than the recipe many clinicians know.
- The bleach method for water. Also quoted exactly. One wrong spoon size matters.
- The medicines pages, and the questions we tell people to ask about equipment.

**A midwife or health visitor**
- The baby feeding guide, especially bottled water and washing bottles when water is short.

**A fire and rescue service**
- The flats pages. Stay put advice is local to each building, and one source page couldn't be re-read today.
- Whether to say more about escape plans and charging batteries.

**A welfare rights adviser**
- The schemes in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, Healthy Start, water tariffs and severe weather beds. These were not re-read in this pass.
- Amounts and dates change every April.

**Not covered by this review**
- Survey figures, the Lancaster account and the radio advice.
- The Polish, Romanian, Punjabi and Urdu wording of the 19 lines changed today. They were translated by the assistant and not checked by a speaker.
- Three practical tips have no official source and are low risk: keep batteries outside the torch, fill the bath when warned, and keep a head torch by the door.
