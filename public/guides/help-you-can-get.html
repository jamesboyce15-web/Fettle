Help you can get: money, credit and free safety equipment

6 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

Rules and amounts change. Check with Citizens Advice or the official pages linked below before you rely on a figure.

There is more help than most people know about. None of it means you've failed. It's there to be used.

Emergency money for essentials

  • England: your council runs the Crisis and Resilience Fund. It started on 1 April 2026 and took over from the Household Support Fund. Where there is a county council, the county runs it. It can help with food, energy and essentials in a crisis, often as cash. Each council runs it its own way: search for your council's name and "Crisis and Resilience Fund", or phone them.
  • Scotland: a crisis grant from the Scottish Welfare Fund. Apply through your council.
  • Wales: an emergency assistance payment from the Discretionary Assistance Fund. Apply through the Welsh Government.
  • Northern Ireland: discretionary support from the Finance Support Service.

Not sure where to start? Citizens Advice can tell you what you can apply for.

Gas and electricity

  • On a prepayment meter: emergency credit, friendly hours, additional support credit and fuel vouchers. See prepayment meters.
  • Behind on bills: tell your supplier. They must work out a payment plan you can afford.

Payments after a long power cut

You can get a payment from your electricity network if your power is off for a long time. Most are paid automatically.

  • In normal weather: £100 if you're off for more than 12 hours (more than 24 hours for a big power cut affecting 5,000 homes or more), then £45 for each extra 12 hours.
  • After a storm: £90 once you've been off for 24 hours, or 48 hours for the worst storms, then £45 for every 6 hours after that.
  • If you're cut off a lot: £100 if your power goes off more than 4 times in a year for 3 hours or more each time. You have to claim this one from your network.

If you think you're owed a payment and nothing has come, contact your network operator. Being on the Priority Services Register doesn't give you a bigger payment, but it does get you extra help during the cut.

Free safety equipment and help

  • Smoke alarms: many fire and rescue services fit them free on a home fire safety visit. Ask yours.
  • If you rent: your landlord must fit and repair alarms. See renting and social housing and temporary accommodation.
  • The Priority Services Register: free, and it gets you updates and practical help in a power or water cut.

Babies and young children

  • Healthy Start (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) and Best Start Foods (Scotland) help pay for milk, infant formula, fruit and vegetables if you're pregnant or have a young child and get certain benefits. Ask your midwife or health visitor.
  • Baby banks give nappies and essentials free. Your health visitor can refer you.

Water

If you struggle to pay your water bill, ask your water company about its cheaper tariff for people on a low income. In England and Wales, every company has one.
