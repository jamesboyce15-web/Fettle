Renting: what your landlord must do, and what you can do

4 minute read · Last checked 7 November 2026 ·

If you rent, some of your safety is your landlord's job. Here's what the law says in England, and what you can sort out yourself. The rules are different in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

Alarms: your landlord must fit them

Since 1 October 2022, private and social landlords in England must:

  • fit a smoke alarm on every storey of your home that has a room used as living accommodation
  • fit a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed fuel-burning appliance, such as a gas boiler or wood burner (gas cookers are excluded)
  • repair or replace an alarm once you tell them it's faulty

Test your alarms once a month by pressing the button. If one doesn't work, tell your landlord in writing, such as by email, and keep a copy.

Gas and electrical safety

  • If you have gas, your landlord must have the appliances checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer and give you a copy of the record.
  • Private landlords must have the electrics inspected at least every 5 years and give you a copy of the report.

Ask your landlord

  • Where is the stopcock, and the fuse box?
  • If I live in a block: what is the fire strategy, and what does the main door do in a power cut?
  • Who do I call for an emergency repair out of hours?

What you can do yourself

  • Keep 3 days of water and food that needs no cooking. It's yours and takes little space.
  • Get contents insurance. Your landlord's insurance doesn't cover your belongings.
  • Keep a torch, a power bank and a battery radio.
  • Join the Priority Services Register if you're eligible. You don't need your landlord's permission.
  • If you live in a flat, read living in a high-rise flat.
