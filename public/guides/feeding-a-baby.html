Feeding a baby when the power or water fails

6 minute read · Last checked 7 November 2026 ·

Draft. This guide is waiting for review by an NHS professional. Always follow advice from your midwife, health visitor, GP or pharmacist, and the official guidance linked below.

Babies can't wait, and powder formula needs water at a temperature you may not be able to reach in a power cut. A small stock of the right things removes most of the worry.

If you're breastfeeding

Breastfeeding needs no power, water or equipment. Keep going, and drink plenty yourself. Breastfeeding women should also avoid chilled ready-to-eat food and leftovers after 4 hours without power.

Ready-made formula is the easiest backup

Ready-to-feed liquid formula comes sterile in a carton or bottle and needs no water or heating. Keep 3 days' worth at home, plus a supply of single-use pre-sterilised bottles or teats, and rotate it before it goes out of date.

  • Work out 3 days: the number of feeds your baby has a day, times the amount per feed, times 3.
  • Once opened, follow the storage instructions on the pack. Without a fridge, use an opened carton straight away.
  • Throw away any milk left in the bottle after a feed.

If you use powder formula

The NHS says to make up powder formula with freshly boiled tap water that's still at least 70°C, which means using it within 30 minutes of boiling. Hot water kills bacteria that can be in the powder.

  • A gas hob may still work in a power cut. Light it with a match.
  • A camping stove is fine outside only, never indoors or on a balcony.
  • A flask of boiled water does not stay hot enough to make powder formula safely.

If the tap water is off or not safe

Bottled water isn't usually recommended for formula, because some contains too much salt (sodium) or sulphate. The Food Standards Agency published guidance in November 2026 on preparing formula when the water supply is disrupted, including how to choose a suitable bottled water. Read the FSA guidance and follow it. During a boil water or do not drink notice, your water company will also give advice for babies.

Sterilising

  • Single-use pre-sterilised bottles avoid the problem entirely.
  • Cold-water sterilising tablets or liquid work without power, but need clean water.
  • Steam sterilisers and microwave sterilisers need power.

Keep baby warm, fed and changed

  • Keep 3 days of nappies and wipes.
  • Dress your baby in layers you can add or remove. Don't overwrap them in bed.
  • If your baby is feeding less, is very sleepy, has fewer wet nappies or seems unwell, call 111. Call 999 in an emergency.

Sort these out now

  1. Buy 3 days of ready-to-feed formula, if you use formula.
  2. Buy a pack of pre-sterilised single-use bottles.
  3. Read the FSA guidance on bottled water for formula, so you know what to look for.
  4. Keep 3 days of nappies and wipes.
  5. Join the Priority Services Register. Families with children under 5 can usually join.
