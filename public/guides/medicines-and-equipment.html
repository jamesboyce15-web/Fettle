Fridge medicines and powered medical equipment

5 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

General information, not medical advice. Always follow advice from your midwife, health visitor, GP or pharmacist, and the official guidance linked below. If you are worried, call NHS 111.

If someone at home relies on a medicine kept in the fridge, or equipment that runs on mains power, a power cut is more than an inconvenience. Most of the planning is a few phone calls, made now.

Medicines kept in the fridge

Common examples include insulin, some eye drops, some injections and some liquid antibiotics. How long each one can safely stay out of the fridge varies a lot.

Ask your pharmacist, for each fridge medicine:

  • How long can it stay at room temperature and still work?
  • What should I do with it if the fridge is off for longer?
  • Can I get a cool bag or travel case for it?

Write the answers down and keep them with the medicine. The leaflet in the box often says too.

  • In a power cut, keep the fridge shut, and take the medicine out only when you need it.
  • Most fridge medicines must not freeze: check the leaflet. If you use ice packs, wrap them so they don't touch the medicine.
  • Keep taking medicines at the usual times. If a dose is missed or a medicine may have spoiled, ask a pharmacist or call 111.

Equipment that needs power

This includes oxygen concentrators, home dialysis, ventilators and suction machines, feeding pumps, hoists and stairlifts, electric profiling beds, and pressure-relief air mattresses.

Ask your equipment supplier:

  • Does it have a backup battery, and how long does it last?
  • What should we do if the power is off for longer than that? (For oxygen, ask about backup cylinders.)
  • For a hoist or bed: can it be lowered or moved by hand?
  • For an air mattress: what happens to it without power, and what should we do?
  • What's your 24-hour number?

Get on the Priority Services Register

If you rely on medical equipment or fridge medicines, you can join your energy and water companies' Priority Services Register for free. They'll know about you during a cut, and may give you advance warning of planned ones.

If life is at risk

If equipment fails and someone's breathing or life is at risk, call 999. Don't wait for the power to come back.

Sort these out now

  1. Ask the pharmacist how long each fridge medicine can be out of the fridge. Write it down.
  2. Ask each equipment supplier the questions above, and save their 24-hour number on paper.
  3. Don't run low. Order repeat prescriptions in good time, about a week before they run out, so there are always a few days in hand. Keep a written list of each medicine and its dose.
  4. Charge any backup batteries and power banks.
  5. Join the Priority Services Register.
