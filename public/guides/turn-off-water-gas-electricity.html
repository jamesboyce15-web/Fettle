Find and turn off your water, gas and electricity

4 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

A burst pipe, a gas smell or a flood warning gives you minutes, not hours. Find these three things now, while the lights are on, and show everyone at home.

Water: the stop tap

  • Where: most inside stop taps are under the kitchen sink. Otherwise look under the stairs, in an airing cupboard, a downstairs toilet, a utility room or the garage.
  • Off: turn it clockwise. It may take a few minutes for the water to stop.
  • On: turn it anticlockwise.
  • Try it now, slowly. Never force it. If it's stiff or won't turn, ask a plumber to look at it before you need it.
  • When: a burst or leaking pipe.

Gas: the emergency control valve

  • Where: next to the gas meter. That can be under the stairs, in the kitchen or garage, in a box outside the front, side or back of the house, or in a shared cupboard in a block of flats. An outside box needs its key: find it now.
  • Off: the lever is normally in line with the pipe. Turn it a quarter turn, so it sits across the pipe.
  • Don't turn it back on until you're told it's safe.
  • When: you smell gas, or you're told to. Open doors and windows. Don't use light switches or anything that could spark, and don't light anything. Call 0800 111 999, free, any time.

Electricity: the main switch

  • Where: the consumer unit, or fuse box. Often in a hall cupboard, under the stairs, by the front door or in the garage.
  • Off: the main switch is at one end, usually red and labelled "main switch". Switch it down to turn off all the electricity.
  • One switch has clicked down on its own? That circuit has tripped. Push it back up. If it trips again, unplug everything on that circuit and plug them back in one at a time to find the one causing it.
  • Never touch it with wet hands, or if you're standing in water.
  • Older fuse boxes may have only a main switch, or none. If yours looks nothing like this, ask your landlord or an electrician to show you.
  • When: a flood is coming into the house and you can reach it safely, or you think there's an electrical fault.

In a flood

If you get a flood warning, the government advises turning off your gas, electricity and water if it's safe to do so. Don't touch an electrical switch if you're standing in water.

Do this now

  • Find all three. Take a photo of each on your phone.
  • Show everyone at home who's old enough.
  • Gently try the stop tap.
  • Find the key for an outside meter box, and keep a torch nearby.
  • Write where they are in your workbook, or on your Fettle plan.
