Which radio to keep for a power cut

3 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

If the power, phones and internet all go down, radio is how official advice would reach you. The government's plan is for the UK's Chief Medical Officers to broadcast health advice on BBC Radio 2 and Radio 4, repeated regularly.

Get a radio with FM

  • BBC Radio 4 stopped broadcasting on long wave in June 2026. An old long wave set won't pick it up.
  • Choose a radio with FM. FM carries Radio 2, Radio 4 and your BBC local station, which has local news.
  • Pick one that runs on ordinary AA or AAA batteries, and keep spares. A wind-up or solar radio is a good backup, because it never runs out.
  • A car radio works too, but never run the engine in a closed garage.

Why FM

When Lancaster lost power for four days in 2015, FM radio was one of only two things that kept working, along with old copper landlines. The BBC's emergency plan keeps its FM transmitters running on diesel generators for at least three days. What Lancaster teaches us

Write these down now

In a power cut, you can't look up the frequencies. Tune in now and write on a label stuck to the radio:

  • BBC Radio 2 on FM: ______
  • BBC Radio 4 on FM: ______
  • Your BBC local radio station on FM: ______

Keep a pen and paper with it

The broadcast scripts begin by suggesting you write the advice down. Keep a notepad and pen with the radio. Fettle's book has a page for these notes.

Test it each season

Turn it on, check the batteries haven't leaked, and check it still finds your stations. Keep spare batteries in their packet next to the radio, not inside it.
