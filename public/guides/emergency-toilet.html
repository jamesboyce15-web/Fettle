Using the toilet and washing hands when the water is off

4 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

It sounds strange, and nobody wants to think about it. But it's one of the first problems in any long water cut, and a few minutes now means you'll know exactly what to do.

First: are the sewers still working?

What you do depends on this. Your water company or the radio will tell you.

  • Only your water is off, as in a burst main. The sewers usually still work, so you can flush by hand (below).
  • A national power cut, or you're told the sewers aren't working. Don't flush at all. Use a bin bag in the toilet (below).

If only the water is off: flush with a bucket

Pour a bucket of water quickly into the bowl. Use bath water or water that isn't safe to drink, never your stored drinking water.

If you're told not to flush: a bin bag in the toilet

The UK Chief Medical Officers' advice for a national power cut is not to flush, and to collect waste in plastic bags, seal them and put them in your normal household waste, ideally an outside bin.

  1. Lift the seat. Put a strong bin bag in the bowl, with the edges over the rim.
  2. Put the seat back down. It holds the bag in place. Use the toilet as normal.
  3. Lift the bag out and tie it tightly. Put it in an outside bin if you have one.
  4. Wash your hands in the shared bowl (below). Put a new bag in.

Don't try to flush, even with a bucket. Without power, the sewers can't take it.

Washing hands: one shared bowl

  • Fill a washing-up bowl with water and add soap or household disinfectant.
  • Everyone shares it for the whole time the water is off, even when it stops looking clean. The Chief Medical Officers say it stays safe for hand-washing.
  • Anyone with diarrhoea or sickness uses their own separate bowl.
  • Wash your hands after the toilet and before preparing or eating food.
  • Soap and water works better than hand sanitiser. Use sanitiser only if you have nothing else.

Keep these together, now

  • A roll of strong bin bags, kept by the toilet
  • Two washing-up bowls, one marked for anyone who's ill
  • Soap or household disinfectant, and hand sanitiser as a backup
  • Toilet roll and kitchen roll
