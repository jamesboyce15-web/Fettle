Guides · Money and support

For food banks, kitchens and community groups

6 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

Rules and amounts change. Check with Citizens Advice or the official pages linked below before you rely on a figure.

People on the lowest incomes are the least likely to be prepared, and the most likely to say cost is why. You already have their trust. A few small things from you can make a real difference.

Five things you can do, most of them free

  1. Do the Fettle check with someone. It takes a few minutes on your phone or theirs. They don't need an account or an email address. Read the questions out if that's easier.
  2. Help people join the Priority Services Register. It's the single most useful free thing. You can do it with them in a few minutes.
  3. Add no-cook food to parcels when storms are forecast: ring-pull tins, cereal bars, crackers, long-life milk. Ask "can you heat food at home at the moment?" as a routine question.
  4. Keep fuel voucher and crisis fund details to hand. See help you can get and prepayment meters.
  5. Hand out one sheet of paper with the numbers below, and space to write in a neighbour's or family member's number.

If the people you support have nowhere stable to live

  • In severe cold, councils open emergency beds for people sleeping rough. This is called SWEP, the Severe Weather Emergency Protocol. Each council decides when to open it, usually when it's forecast to freeze. Find out now who to phone at your council.
  • If you're worried about someone sleeping rough in England or Wales, you can tell StreetLink, which passes it to the local outreach team. If someone is in danger, call 999.
  • For people in hostels, B&Bs or sofa-surfing, see social housing and temporary accommodation.

Is your own building ready?

  • Could you open in a power cut? Think about light, a way to boil water, and a phone that works.
  • Do you know which of your regulars live alone, and who would check on them?
  • Could you be a warm, lit place for a few hours? Tell your council's emergency planning team: they may want to know.

If you want to do more

Fettle for Communities lets staff and volunteers do checks with people, keep their plans with consent, and see, without names, what the people you support are missing and what gets in their way. Ask us about it.
