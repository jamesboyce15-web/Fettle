Keeping cool in a heatwave when the power is off

4 minute read · Last checked 12 November 2026 ·

Summers are getting hotter. A power cut in a heatwave means no fans and no cold drinks from the fridge, and heat is dangerous for babies, young children, pregnant women, older people and anyone who needs care.

Keep the home cool

  • During the day, close windows, blinds and curtains that face the sun. Close outside shutters if you have them.
  • Open windows only when it's cooler outside than in, usually in the evening and at night.
  • Sit and sleep in the coolest room.
  • If you have to go out for water, food or to check on someone, go at the coolest time of day.

Drink more

Drink at least 2 litres a day, about 8 cups, and more in the heat. Store 3 litres per person per day so there's enough.

Signs of heat exhaustion

Tiredness, weakness, feeling faint, a headache, muscle cramps, feeling or being sick, heavy sweating and intense thirst.

Signs of heatstroke

Heat exhaustion can turn into heatstroke, which is an emergency. Look for confusion, clumsiness, a fast heartbeat, fast or shallow breathing, hot skin, and fits (seizures).

What to do

  1. Move them indoors or into the shade.
  2. Take off extra clothing, such as jackets and socks.
  3. Give them water, a fizzy drink or another drink.
  4. Spray or sponge them with cool water. It doesn't need to be drinking water, so don't use the water you've made safe to drink.
  5. They should start to cool down and feel better within 30 minutes.
  6. Call 999 if they're still unwell after 30 minutes, or if they show any sign of heatstroke. While you wait, keep cooling them.

Check on others

Look out for neighbours, friends and family who may be struggling, especially older people living alone. See checking on someone.
